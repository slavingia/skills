The minimalist entrepreneur Before I started to write this book, I wouldn’t have described myself as a minimalist entrepreneur , mostly because I had never heard of such a thing. If you had asked me, I would have said I was a founder committed to a new kind of startup, one that prioritized profitability over growth and positive impact over moving fast and breaking things. Mostly , I was determined to create value for our customers and our team. But in the years since I changed course with Gumroad, I noticed that more and more friends, colleagues, and acquaintances were taking the same approach to building their own companies. People that you’ll meet in this book like Peter Askew or Laura Roeder of MeetEdgar and Paperbell. W e exchanged ideas on Twitter or at conferences, and the more people I talked to, the more I felt we should label this new pathway to success, one that isn’t dependent on flashy growth or unsustainable metrics. One that uses the advent of software to democratize business creation for everyone. Updating the status quo has always been the true function of entrepreneurship. That’ s been my goal since the beginning even if achieving that goal has taken shape in a way I could never have imagined. But now I see that if everyone knew that being a minimalist entrepreneur was an option, I suspect a whole lot more of us would have pursued this path from the start.
