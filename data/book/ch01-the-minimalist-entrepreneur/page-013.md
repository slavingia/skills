● PROFIT ABILITY - Minimalist entrepreneurs create businesses that are profitable at all costs. Many businesses never intend to stick around long enough to be profitable. Instead, the plan is to sell the business before profits become necessary , raising money from investors along the way . Minimalist entrepreneurs aim to be profitable from day one or soon after because profit is oxygen. And you do that by selling a product, not by collecting and selling user data. From the start, you should be selling to your users, not selling your users. ● COMMUNITY - Minimalist entrepreneurs put community first. Be observant and cultivate authentic relationships. Eventually you’ll notice one of your communities stubbing its toe on a problem it just can’t solve. That’ s your business idea. Or you can solve your own problem and offer the solution to real people in the real communities you belong to. Since you’ve taken time and ef fort to build trust at the outset, you’ll have a much easier time selling to your first customers once you reach product-market fit. It’ s much cheaper , too. ● PROCESS - Minimalist entrepreneurs build only what they need to, automating or outsourcing the rest. Minimalist businesses do one thing and do it well. You assume your customers are competent and able to articulate their own problems. Then, you work with them to come up with the best possible solution, and make sure it’ s worth paying for , before you take it to customers outside your initial communities. Most importantly , never over-build. 13
