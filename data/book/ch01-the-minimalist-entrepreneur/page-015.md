[head A] Chase profitability , not unicorns One of the keys to understanding the minimalist entrepreneur mindset is embracing profitability , not growth, as the key indicator of your company’ s success. Building a minimalist business may not be a get rich quick proposition, but it is a get rich slowly one if you use this one principle as a north star for your decision making. Profitability means sustainability . It doesn’t mean treading water until a lifeboat comes along to save you, which is how many founders I know think about raising their next round of VC funding. It means building your own boat. And while I do think the minimalist entrepreneur mindset leads to a near-100% success rate, I’m willing to concede that it may only happen over the course of many experiments. That’ s why profitability is really the only metric that matters. If you’re profitable, you can take many shots on goal and maximize your chance of getting to a place where you can take unlimited shots on goal, virtually guaranteeing your success as long as you keep learning from and building for your customers. I’m optimistic that we’re moving past an era of gatekeeping, where bosses and universities and venture capitalists decide who gets to try and who doesn’t. Information about how to start and scale companies is now available to founders around the world, and there are fewer and fewer reasons to raise venture capital. That’ s a great thing! Every time founders go that route, raise money , and trade away partial ownership of their companies, they take a big step away from building true success in the form of sustainable, profitable businesses based on deep customer knowledge and solid financials. Some eventually make their way back, years later, but many never do. Instead, the companies die and the customers are left abandoned. Why does this happen? The venture capital business is a high-risk, high-return investment strategy in which venture funds swap capital for early-stage startup equity , essentially buying a piece of the future value of the companies in which they invest.
