Build a manual valuable process first [epi] Make something people want. – Y Combinator 42 Make something people want. – Me The previous chapter was all about finding a problem worth solving for people worth solving for . Now we’re going to talk about zeroing in on the smallest stumbling block imaginable, creating a simple, repeatable solution, and building it in a scalable way . In this chapter , I explain how to develop your idea and, more importantly , how to figure out what you need to do now versus what can wait until you’re in business. Knowledge is important, but so is momentum. You don’t want to get so in the weeds on which programming language to learn that you never start making your dream app. Especially at the beginning, minimalist entrepreneurs have to stick to what is truly essential rather than trying to learn and do everything all at once. While writers are often told to “write what you know ,” for entrepreneurs, the process isn’t so simple. When you’re starting a business, you’re often imagining something—a product, a service, a business model—that’ s never been tried before. That said, most successful minimalist entrepreneurs have a solid background (or interest) in one aspect of the business they’re starting even if they don’t know everything about it or exactly how to begin. For me, it was simple: pretty , accessible software. When the iPhone App Store launched in 2008, I was one of its first wave of developers. Not because I was determined to start a business, but because I was following my passions and curiosity . Unfortunately , in my conversations with aspiring founders, this point is where most folks decide that building a business is not for them. They have the passion, but they let self-doubt creep in, persuading themselves that they don’t possess the hard skills they think they need, such as iOS programming or financial modeling. But remember , many entrepreneurs have built successful mobile apps without knowing how to program on day one. This is about interests, not skills. Instead of focusing on the things you do not know , focus on the things you do. You do not need a team, money , or a degree to start building. You don’t need to ship or to code to make your idea come to life – at first .
