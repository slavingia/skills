Like me, Ryan doesn’t believe that founders should start with code. “Do shitty work people love at first,” he says. As more and more infrastructure gets built by new businesses (including, perhaps, the one you’re working on now), it is getting cheaper , faster , and more accessible to build a MVP without code. What that means is that you shouldn’t wait until tomorrow to get started. The lower the barriers to entry , the more competition you will have. The trendline is simple: democratization. Everything that a software engineer can do today , everyone can do tomorrow . It means you need to know less to do more. Even if your service is manual, or your product is physical, you will be able to take advantage of software to provide your service as ef ficiently as possible. Every single business is in some way tech-enabled, even though the end product may not be. For example, if you are building a software business, you can visit Makerpad and learn how to connect Gumroad and Carrd to accept orders on your website without writing a single line of code. And when you are ready to automate your manual fulfillment process, it will teach you how to add Airtable and Google Forms and Mailchimp.There are products like Notion, which we use to run our entire company . And there are services like Zapier , which allow you to automate the connections between all the software you use. Similar to processizing your workflow as you were helping people, these tools will let you processize and later productize the internal functions of your business itself. Perhaps most importantly , they will save you money . The further you can get without hiring your first engineer if you are building a software product, the higher your chances of achieving profitability . And the further you get, the better the employee you can hire. (And more often than you think, these people will find you!) [head A] Ship early and often Building a business is a lesson in fast feedback loops and iteration. Imagine if you were on a boat searching for treasure, but you could only ping your radar once a year . Then, once a month. Then, every day . The boat is your business, and the treasure is product-market fit. You will be wrong a lot; the goal is to get less wrong as quickly as you can. This is why shipping early and often is so important. Gumroad, for example, has never shipped a “v2” in nine years. Instead, we have shipped tens of thousands (literally) of incremental and major improvements over time. Each time, we cross the threshold for some customer from “I may want this later” to “I need this now.” 65
