Even when you conquer community , you’ll still have self-doubt about product. When you build and ship a product, you’ll have self-doubt about sales. When you’ve done everything mentioned in this book, you’ll have self-doubt about whether you’re qualified enough to write it all down. (Hi!) After stepping down as CEO of Product Hunt in 2020, Ryan Hoover found himself thinking more about the conditions that made it possible for him to build Product Hunt and how it might be possible to help squash self-doubt for others and to create the same kind of conditions for the founders he works with through W eekend Fund, the early stage startup fund he launched in 2017. For him, it comes down to four things: validation, mentorship, time, and accountability . In the early stages, even though you’re probably laser focused on your product, you can still set yourself up for success, whether you’re going it alone or working with a co-founder , whether you’re bootstrapping or you have investors. If you’ve productized, your first customers have hopefully validated the utility of your of fering, but you’ll also sometimes want or even need someone you respect to give you the confidence to move forward and to vanquish imposter syndrome. That’ s where a trusted mentor comes in. Traditionally , a mentor is an older , wiser person who’s already done what you’re trying to do, but a mentor can be anyone who of fers suggestions and helps with aspects of your business that aren’t so easy for you. “W e tend to get wrapped up in our own heads,” Ryan says, “and sometimes all it takes is ten minutes of talking to someone else to uncover a solution.” You also want to protect the time you need to grow your idea. No matter what stage of life you’re in, there will always be demands on your time, and when your company is a side project, it may grow more slowly than you like. There will be weeks or months when other priorities jump ahead of building your product, but don’t use that as an excuse to lose forward momentum, even if it’ s invisible to everyone but you. When I first launched Gumroad, I was full-time at Pinterest, and I dedicated every other weekend to shipping at least one new feature. The other weekend I reserved for everything else. You might be able to do that on your own, but if you can’t, your community , which has supported and inspired you up until now , can also serve as a source of accountability whether through in-person “co-working” meetings, a Slack channel, a course you’re participating in, or yes, a customer you’ve made a promise to. Just like you used processization to build your product, you can also use processization to set yourself up to grow with your company . Just get going. Your failures will fade, while your successes will stick around and compound. You didn’t believe you’d get this far , yet you did. Just remind yourself of that as you keep going. 67
