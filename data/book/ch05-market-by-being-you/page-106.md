Just like you don't want to rely on social media companies to mediate your relationships to your customers, you don't want your business model to depend on outside companies providing you with af fordable advertising. The sooner your marketing function is as sustainable as the rest of your business, the better . And there’ s no better time to start than from the very beginning. [head A] Spend money on your customers Instead of thinking about loyalty programs as a marketing function, think of them like genuine rewards to loyal customers. Startups like PayPal and Uber spend millions of dollars paying their users to help them grow . But this may pose an obstacle to real growth. If people are only sharing your product because they are being financially rewarded for it, it is unsustainable. Instead, you can use loyalty programs to encourage repeat visits, or of fer discounts for leaving a business review online or for sharing it on social media. But don’t forget a common sense rule: don’t pay more than you make. Eventually , you can worry about getting headlines. Once you have a business with customers, getting coverage becomes much easier . At that point, you can go beyond the outreach to community reporters and micro-influencers that I mentioned in Chapter 4. Now you’re ready to of fer your product for free to reviewers and more established influencers, or you can of fer samples of your products or exclusive information on your company to bloggers and journalists who cover your space. But most of all, you can just tell your story . You can be yourself. You worked hard for this. You struggled. You can show others that their hard work and struggles will be rewarded too. Helena Hambrecht, co-founder and co-CEO of Haus, an aperitif brand, sees enormous potential in enlisting your best customers as marketers. This is one of the strategies she and her husband, W oody , a third generation winemaker , are using to market their natural, lower alcohol by volume spirits which they sell direct-to-consumer .
