To be clear , this isn’t about scaling back your ambitions in order to make your business work. Because I’m not trying to build a billion-dollar business at all costs, my focus is on creating more creators and business owners. And frankly , you often can’t grow faster if you try . I’ve worked 60 hours a week for years on end, and I’ve worked four hours a week. For better or worse, Gumroad grew at its own pace, and the number of hours I worked didn't seem to have much of a correlation. I think you’ll find the same is true for you: your company will grow as quickly as your customers determine it will grow . For us, that was 15% in 2017, 25% in 2018, 40% in 2019, and 87% year-over-year in 2020. It taught me to be wary of thinking I always needed to do more, earn more, or grow more than I needed to. Once I came to terms with the reality that I couldn’t control everything, it got a lot easier to move forward. Instead of pretending to be a product visionary and trying to build a billion-dollar company , as if it were within my control, I could focus on making Gumroad better for our existing creators. Some say that you need to grow like crazy , because “if you don’t get big, someone else will eat you.” As if companies were fish. This is wrong. The vast majority of small businesses are never eaten. Big fish want to eat other big fish, generally . In fact, the longest lived businesses in the world are also some of the smallest. They are restaurants, hotels, construction companies, and more. Many of them are family firms, or small to mid-sized enterprises content with steady evolution of their niche and a passionate multi-generational customer base. Something to aspire to! Maybe you already know this. Maybe that’ s the business you already aspire to build. If so, I’m glad. But it wasn’t obvious to me when I started out, and I see these ideologies pervade and persist in social media, within headlines, and on TV. One more economics lesson to wrap up: there’ s no free lunch. Once you have it, you will feel the pressure to spend money more loosely . Keep in mind the lessons we covered in this chapter as you start to spend your customers’ money , making sure you’re treating it like your own. It may be tempting to solve your problems with money–and people–but that usually only exacerbates things. Instead of hiring like crazy , hire when it hurts. This way you’ll have a more mature business for new folks to fit into. Plus, you’ll minimize the whiplash that comes with building a rapidly changing business with a bunch of revolving faces. 127
