Chapter 8. Where do we go from here? [epi] We are born to wander through a chaos field. And yet we do not become hopelessly lost, because each walker who comes before us leaves behind a trace for us to follow . 90 Robert Moor This chapter comes last, but I rank it first in significance. Even after reading this book, you may be asking yourself if now is the right time to start a company . There is a lot broken with the world, and the future is uncertain. Starting a business may look risky . And it is and likely always will be. But I believe it’ s one of the best ways to make change. Never mind if your business is not solving the “big” issues from day one, or doesn’t employ hundreds of people. As long as you’re making the world better in an honest, scalable way by selling a product worth paying for to a community that wants it, starting a company is worth it. I don’t think healing the world only happens if we are able to make “a dent in the universe,” as Steve Jobs is famously misquoted; it also comes about by repeatedly making small choices that compound and that improve our communities. You can’t change everything, but you can and should change a few things, to start. The reward, once you’re profitable and growing sustainably because your customers are spreading the word, is that you get to decide what your company’ s next positive impact will be. I know that once Gumroad got to that point, it was easier for me to focus on a more meaningful life. But it wasn’t easy . I had to wrestle with a new definition of success, one that would be defined and underpinned by a greater sense of purpose and mission. “A healthy man wants a thousand things, a sick man only wants one,” Confucius is purported to have said. Rephrased in the context of this book, a minimalist 91 entrepreneur without a successful, sustainable business only wants one thing (that!), while one who has achieved it has the world as their oyster .
