[head A] Create more creators, CEOs, and minimalist entrepreneurs After two years in Provo, I had a new perspective on Gumroad and my mission with respect to the company . When I reimagined the future, I realized I had the chance to not only make a life for myself but also, more importantly , to expand opportunities for others to make a life for themselves. Building Gumroad exposed me to a whole new range of creators: business owners. Not every act of creation requires a business, but many creators who reach scale start a business to manage their work. One of the things I’m proudest of is that I’ve made the business side of creation easier for thousands of other people and created business owners around the world. In 2020, I stumbled upon the next step in my journey to make entrepreneurship accessible to all. Up until then, I had experimented in investing in early-stage tech startups by becoming a Limited Partner in Arlan Hamilton’ s Backstage Capital and making several small angel investments in startups such as Lambda School, Figma, and Notion. But in the wake of the George Floyd protests in 2020, I knew I could do more, and I tweeted about wanting to invest in Black founders. That tweet demonstrates the power of an audience. It led to 200 emails from Black founders and–most importantly–four new investments in startups founded by Black entrepreneurs. Since then, there have been several more through in-network referrals. But many of these startups were looking to raise more capital, and I didn’t have very much. So I wrote a “memo” and emailed other investors in my network. One responded, “you should start a fund” and of fered to anchor it to help get it of f the ground. So I doubled down on my goal of creating more CEOs and minimalist entrepreneurs and launched my own fund last year . Even though I never expected to be a venture capitalist myself, I am now able to support the kind of founders I meet primarily through my audience and through building Gumroad openly and transparently . Today I get to invest approximately $10 million dollars a year in about 50 companies. I still turn many founders away , but that’ s not necessarily because I don’t believe in what they’re doing. It’ s because most businesses would be better of f without venture capital. Ever since I published “Reflecting on My Failure to Build a Billion-Dollar Company” in February 2019, I’ve met hundreds of minimalist entrepreneurs who match my mental model for what a business really is. And even though I am an investor now , I also want to create more of those too! (Can’t you tell?) 152
