# Find Community — Gotchas

Common failure modes when identifying communities. Update this file when new failures are discovered.

## Community Selection

- **Choosing community for market size, not genuine interest** — leads to burnout within months. If you wouldn't hang out there for free, don't build a business there.
- **Too broad an audience** ("everyone who uses the internet") — you need a niche you can dominate. If you can't describe the community in one sentence, it's too broad.
- **Confusing network with community** — Twitter followers are a network (broadcast). A community has shared interests + relationships + reciprocity. Networks don't buy; communities do.

## Engagement Traps

- **Lurking does not equal membership** — you must be actively contributing before you can sell. If nobody knows your name, you're not a member yet.
- **Jumping straight to "what can I sell"** before understanding the community's real pain — this is the fastest way to get ignored or rejected.
- **Trying to invent a community from scratch** rather than joining existing ones — communities take years to build organically. Join first, build products second.

## Research Pitfalls

- **Exa searches return companies, not communities** — filter results specifically for forums, Discords, subreddits, Slack groups, and mailing lists. Company results tell you about the market, not the community.
- **Community size paradox** — too small = no market, too large = can't stand out. Sweet spot: you can name 50-100 active members and recognize regulars.
