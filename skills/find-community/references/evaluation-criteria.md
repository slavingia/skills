# Community Evaluation Criteria

Detailed framework for scoring and selecting communities to build a minimalist business around.

## The 4-Question Framework (Deep Dive)

### 1. What communities are you already part of?

Think broadly across all life domains:

- **Professional**: Industry groups, Slack workspaces, conferences, meetups, professional associations
- **Hobby**: Sports leagues, gaming communities, creative circles, maker spaces
- **Online**: Reddit, Discord servers, Facebook groups, Substacks, YouTube comment communities, forums
- **Local**: Neighborhood groups, parent groups, religious communities, co-working spaces
- **Identity**: Alumni networks, cultural groups, disability communities, age-based groups
- **Cause**: Non-profits, volunteer orgs, activist groups, open-source projects

The key test: would these people recognize your name?

### 2. Where do you spend time online?

Map actual behavior, not aspirational behavior:

- Which tabs are always open?
- Which apps do you check first in the morning?
- Where do you comment, post, or reply (not just scroll)?
- Which newsletters do you actually read?
- Which group chats are you most active in?

### 3. What problems do you hear repeatedly?

Listen for recurring complaints, workarounds, and frustrations:

- "I wish there was a way to..."
- "Every time I try to X, I end up having to Y..."
- "Why doesn't someone just build..."
- Manual processes people have invented (spreadsheets, Notion templates, email chains)
- Money being spent on bad solutions (overpriced tools, consultants, agencies)

### 4. Which communities would you serve for years?

The sustainability filter:

- Would you attend this community's events even if your business failed?
- Do you genuinely care about these people's problems?
- Can you see yourself becoming a respected elder/leader here?
- Is the community growing, stable, or declining?

## Community Scoring Criteria

Rate each candidate community 1-5 on these dimensions:

| Criterion | 1 (Weak) | 5 (Strong) |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| **Genuine membership** | You lurk occasionally | You're a recognized contributor |
| **Pain severity** | Mild inconvenience | People actively spend money on bad solutions |
| **Reachability** | No idea where they gather | You know the forums, events, and leaders by name |
| **Size sweet spot** | <50 or >100K active members | 500-10K active, you can name 50-100 |
| **Personal energy** | Feels like work | You'd do it for free |
| **Willingness to pay** | "That would be nice" | "Take my money" |

**Threshold**: Score of 20+ across all six = strong candidate. Below 15 = move on.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Fix |
|-------------|-------------|-----|
| Market-first thinking | "Crypto is hot, I'll build for crypto traders" — no genuine connection, no insight edge | Start from communities you're already in |
| Survey-driven validation | Surveys measure hypothetical intent, not real behavior | Talk to 10 people. Watch what they do, not what they say. |
| Platform as community | "Instagram users" is a platform, not a community | Drill down: which subculture on Instagram? Which hashtag tribe? |
| Community tourism | Joining 15 communities for a week each | Go deep in 2-3, not wide in 15 |
| Solving your own problem only | "I need this so others must too" — sample size of 1 | Validate that at least 10 others have the same problem |
| Ignoring existing solutions | "Nobody is solving this" — almost certainly false | Map the competitive landscape. Understand why existing solutions fall short. |

## Output Template

After evaluation, present findings in this format:

### Community Shortlist

| Community | Persistent Problem | Your Connection | Where They Gather |
|-----------|--------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| e.g., Indie SaaS founders | Can't get early customers without a marketing budget | Active on IndieHackers, ship products monthly | IndieHackers, r/SaaS, MicroConf, Twitter #buildinpublic |

### For Each Finalist

**Community**: [Name]
**Score**: [X/30]
**Problem statement**: [One sentence describing the persistent pain]
**Evidence**: [3 specific examples of people expressing this pain — links, quotes, threads]
**Existing solutions**: [What people currently use, and why it falls short]
**Your edge**: [Why you specifically can serve this community better]
**Next actions**:
1. [Specific person to talk to]
2. [Specific gathering to attend or engage in]
3. [Specific problem to validate through conversation]
