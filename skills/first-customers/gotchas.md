# Gotchas — First Customers

Common failure modes when helping users find their first customers.

## Launching before 100 customers

"Launch" is a celebration of your community's support, not a customer acquisition strategy. If you launch to get customers, you've already lost. Get 100 paying customers first, then throw a party.

## Copy-paste cold emails

Each email must be personalized. The template in `references/cold-outreach-template.md` is a starting point, not a fill-in-the-blank. Every send should reference something specific about the recipient — what they're working on, a post they wrote, a problem they mentioned.

## Treating sales as dirty

Users often resist selling because it feels sleazy. Reframe immediately: you built something that helps people. Not telling them about it is the selfish move. Sales is education, not manipulation.

## Waiting for inbound instead of doing outbound

Manual, outbound sales accounts for 99% of early growth. There is no viral loop, no growth hack, no content strategy that replaces personally reaching out to potential customers one by one. Inbound comes later, after word of mouth builds.

## Asking for social media posts instead of honest feedback

Early customers are more valuable as feedback sources than as promoters. Ask "what would make this worth paying for?" not "can you tweet about this?" Social proof comes naturally from a product that actually works.

## Skipping friends and family because it's uncomfortable

This is the most common avoidance pattern. Friends and family are Circle 1 for a reason — they trust you the most. If you can't sell to people who already like you, cold outreach will be brutal. The discomfort is the point; it's practice for harder conversations.

## Exa prospect search returns companies, not people

When using `web_search_exa` to find prospects, remember: you need to find the PERSON (decision maker), not just the company. A company name is useless without knowing who to email. Always follow up company discovery with finding the right contact — check LinkedIn, team pages, blog authors.

## Trying to sell to everyone instead of selling outward from closest circle

The concentric circles model exists because conversion rates drop as social distance increases. Selling to strangers before exhausting friends, family, and community is inefficient and demoralizing. Work outward systematically.
