# Cold Outreach Template

Sahil Lavingia's actual cold email approach from building Gumroad, plus guidance on customization.

## Sahil's Template

> Hi [Name],
>
> I saw you're selling [what they sell] on your website using [current method], and [pain point you observed]. I built a service called [product] which basically automates all of this. I'd love to show it to you, or you can check it out yourself: [link].
>
> Also happy to just share any learnings we see from [their type of user] in a little [resource you have].
>
> Let me know!
>
> Best,
> [Your name]

### Why This Works

- **Observation first**: Shows you actually looked at what they do
- **Pain point identified**: Proves you understand their problem
- **Low-pressure offer**: "I'd love to show it to you" OR "check it out yourself"
- **Value-add regardless of sale**: Offers learnings/resource even if they don't buy
- **Short**: Under 100 words. Respects their time.

## Customization Guide

Every cold email must include three personalized elements:

### 1. The Observation

Something specific you noticed about the recipient. Not "I saw your website" — that's generic. Instead:

- "I saw you're selling your photography presets through a PayPal button on your blog"
- "I noticed your team posts weekly analytics reports to Slack manually"
- "I read your post about struggling with invoice tracking for freelance clients"

The observation proves you did homework. It earns the rest of the email being read.

### 2. The Pain Point

Connect your observation to a problem your product solves. Be specific:

- "...and it looks like buyers have to email you to get the download link"
- "...which probably takes someone an hour each week to compile"
- "...and it sounds like chasing payments is eating into your actual work time"

### 3. The Soft Close

Never hard-sell in a cold email. Offer two paths:

- A demo/walkthrough (high engagement)
- A self-serve option (low engagement)
- A free resource (no engagement required — builds goodwill)

## Iteration Advice

### After Every Send

Ask yourself:
- Did I reference something specific about this person? If no, rewrite.
- Would I read this if I received it? If no, shorten.
- Is the value proposition clear in one sentence? If no, simplify.

### After Every Response (or Non-Response)

- **Positive reply**: What resonated? Use that angle more.
- **Negative reply**: Was it the wrong person, or the wrong pitch? Adjust.
- **No reply**: Try a different observation or pain point. Follow up once (max) after 5-7 days.

### Common Mistakes in Cold Emails

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Starting with "I" | Start with their name and an observation about them |
| Listing features | Describe the outcome they get |
| Too long (200+ words) | Cut to under 100 words. Every word must earn its place. |
| Generic subject line | Use their name or something specific: "Your [thing] on [platform]" |
| No clear next step | End with a question or simple ask |
| BCC blast to 50 people | Send individually. Personalize each one. This is not marketing. |

### Volume Expectations

- Expect 10-20% reply rate on well-personalized cold emails
- Expect 1-5% conversion rate from reply to paying customer
- That means ~100 emails to get 1-5 customers
- At 5 emails/day, that is 20 business days for your first handful of customers
- This is normal. This is how it works. There is no shortcut.

### Follow-Up Template

If no reply after 5-7 days, one follow-up is acceptable:

> Hi [Name],
>
> Just bumping this — I genuinely think [product] could save you [specific time/effort]. Happy to do a quick walkthrough or just answer any questions.
>
> No worries if it's not the right fit!
>
> [Your name]

One follow-up. Not two. Not three. Respect the silence.
