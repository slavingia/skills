# Marketing Plan — Gotchas

Common failure modes. Check these before giving advice.

## Timing

- **Marketing before 100 customers** — You need product-market fit first. If the user doesn't have repeat customers, redirect to `/first-customers`. Marketing amplifies what's already working; it can't fix a product nobody wants.

- **Spending money before organic traction** — Spend time first, money last. If your free content doesn't resonate, paid ads won't either. Organic traction proves your message works before you scale it with dollars.

## Platform Strategy

- **Juggling all platforms** — Pick ONE and do it well. Consistency on one platform beats sporadic presence on five. Only add a second platform after the first is working and sustainable.

- **Status updates instead of value** — "What I ate for lunch" won't grow your audience. Every post should educate, inspire, or entertain. If it does none of these, don't post it.

- **Rented land (social media) without owned land (email list)** — Algorithms can change overnight. An Instagram account with 10K followers is worth less than an email list of 500 subscribers. Always build the email list in parallel.

## Content

- **Skipping Level 1 (Educate) to jump to Level 3 (Entertain)** — Entertainment is the hardest content to create well. Start with educational content — if you have 100 customers, you've learned 100 things worth sharing. Build up to inspiration and entertainment as you find your voice.

- **Inconsistent posting schedule** — A monthly newsletter with substance beats daily noise. Pick a cadence you can sustain for 6+ months without burning out. Consistency compounds; bursts don't.

## Tool Usage

- **Content research via Exa returns big-brand examples** — Also search for indie creators and bootstrapped successes. Big-brand strategies (content teams, production budgets) don't translate to solo founders. Search with terms like "indie founder blog" or "bootstrapped startup marketing" alongside broader queries.
