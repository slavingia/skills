# Content Levels Framework

Detailed reference for the three levels of content marketing. Progress through them in order — each level reaches a wider audience but demands more skill.

## Level 1: Educate

The easiest starting point. Share what you know from building your business.

**What to share:**
- Lessons from your first 100 customers
- How you solved a specific problem
- Mistakes you made and what you'd do differently
- Tools, processes, or frameworks you use daily
- Industry knowledge your audience doesn't have

**Examples by format:**

| Format | Example |
|--------|---------|
| Blog post | "5 things I learned from my first 50 customer conversations" |
| Twitter thread | Step-by-step breakdown of how you built a specific feature |
| YouTube video | Screen recording walkthrough of your workflow |
| Newsletter | Weekly roundup of lessons learned that week |
| LinkedIn post | "Here's exactly how we reduced churn by 30%" |

**Why it works:** Educational content builds trust. When people learn from you, they associate you with expertise. Your existing audience engages and broadcasts the best ideas to their networks.

**Quality bar:** It doesn't need to be polished or produced — just consistent and genuinely useful. One actionable insight beats a perfectly designed infographic with no substance.

## Level 2: Inspire

Go beyond teaching into motivating. This requires more vulnerability.

**What to share:**
- Your origin story — why you started this business
- Struggles you're facing right now (build in public)
- Milestones and how they felt (not just the numbers)
- Customer transformation stories
- The vision for where you're heading

**Examples by format:**

| Format | Example |
|--------|---------|
| Blog post | "Month 6: How I almost quit and what changed my mind" |
| Twitter thread | Revenue journey from $0 to $10K MRR with the real emotions |
| Podcast | Interview with a customer about how the product changed their work |
| Newsletter | Monthly "state of the business" with honest numbers |
| Instagram | Behind-the-scenes of your workspace, process, daily routine |

**Why it works:** People connect with journeys, not achievements. Documenting your progress — including the messy parts — creates an emotional bond that educational content alone can't build. Think Gimlet's *StartUp* podcast: raw, real, and wildly popular.

**Quality bar:** Authenticity matters more than production value. A genuine phone-camera video outperforms a slick but hollow corporate video every time.

## Level 3: Entertain

The hardest but most far-reaching. Entertainment is the king of content on every platform.

**What makes content entertaining:**
1. Say something (set up)
2. Establish a pattern (the audience thinks they know where it's going)
3. Break the pattern (the punchline, twist, or surprise)

**Examples by format:**

| Format | Example |
|--------|---------|
| Twitter | "Entrepreneurship: work 60 hours a week so you don't have to work 40 hours a week" |
| YouTube | Comedic take on a common industry frustration |
| TikTok/Reels | Quick skit about a relatable customer pain point |
| Newsletter | Opening with a funny anecdote before the lesson |
| Blog post | Satirical take on industry trends ("A Day in the Life of a SaaS Founder") |

**Why it works:** Entertaining content gets shared beyond your niche. Educational content attracts people already interested in your topic. Entertainment attracts everyone — then your expertise converts them.

**Warning:** Do not start here. Entertainment without credibility (built through Levels 1 and 2) comes across as trying too hard. Build your voice with education and inspiration first.

## Social Media Guide

### Two Accounts Rule

Create two accounts on your primary platform:
- **Personal** — You, the human. Ideas, journey, opinions.
- **Business** — You, the product. Updates, tips, customer stories.

The personal account will almost always grow faster. People follow people, not logos.

### Platform Selection

Pick ONE platform based on where your customers already spend time:

| Platform | Best for | Content type | Posting cadence |
|----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Twitter/X | Tech, SaaS, indie hackers | Short-form text, threads | 3-5x/week |
| LinkedIn | B2B, professional services | Long-form posts, articles | 2-3x/week |
| YouTube | Tutorials, reviews, deep dives | Video (5-20 min) | 1x/week |
| Instagram | Visual products, lifestyle, local | Photos, Reels, Stories | 3-5x/week |
| TikTok | Consumer products, younger audience | Short video (15-60s) | 5-7x/week |
| Reddit | Niche communities, technical products | Comments, posts in subreddits | Daily engagement |

### What NOT to Post

- Status updates about your day (unless they contain a lesson)
- Pure self-promotion ("Buy my product!")
- Negativity about competitors
- Content that doesn't educate, inspire, or entertain

### Build in Public Checklist

Share these regularly:
- [ ] Revenue milestones (with context, not just numbers)
- [ ] Feature launches and the reasoning behind them
- [ ] Customer feedback — both positive and constructive
- [ ] Mistakes and what you learned
- [ ] Tools and processes that work for you
- [ ] Weekly/monthly progress updates

## Email Strategy

### Why Email First

| Metric | Social Media | Email |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| You control the algorithm | No | Yes |
| Survives platform changes | No | Yes |
| Average open rate | 1-5% (organic reach) | 20-40% |
| Conversion rate | 0.1-0.5% | 2-5% |
| Direct revenue attribution | Difficult | Clear |

One email subscriber is worth 10-50x a social media follower.

### Lead Magnet Ideas

Offer something valuable in exchange for an email address:

| Type | Example | Effort |
|------|---------|--------|
| Checklist | "The 10-step launch checklist we used" | Low |
| Template | "Our exact email templates for customer outreach" | Low |
| Guide/PDF | "The complete guide to [your niche topic]" | Medium |
| Mini course | "5-day email course: Learn [skill]" | Medium |
| Tool/calculator | "ROI calculator for [your product category]" | High |
| Free trial/sample | Limited access to your product | High |

### Email Content Framework

Apply the same three levels:

| Level | Email example | Frequency |
|-------|--------------|-----------|
| Educate | Weekly tip or lesson from your business | Weekly |
| Inspire | Monthly "state of the business" update | Monthly |
| Entertain | Funny opening anecdote before the lesson | Sprinkle into any email |

### Recommended Cadence

- **Minimum**: Monthly newsletter (substance over frequency)
- **Sweet spot**: Weekly email with one useful insight
- **Maximum**: 2-3x/week (only if each email delivers clear value)

## Content Calendar Template

A sustainable starting schedule. Adjust based on what works.

| Day | Platform (primary) | Email | Content type |
|-----|-------------------|-------|-------------|
| Monday | Post: Educational tip or lesson | — | Level 1: Educate |
| Tuesday | — | — | Create content for rest of week |
| Wednesday | Post: Build-in-public update | — | Level 2: Inspire |
| Thursday | — | Send weekly newsletter | Level 1: Educate |
| Friday | Post: Engaging/entertaining take | — | Level 3: Entertain |
| Weekend | — | — | Rest. Consistency > burnout. |

### Calendar Rules

1. **Batch creation** — Write 3-5 posts in one sitting rather than daily scrambling
2. **80/20 content mix** — 80% value (educate/inspire), 20% promotion
3. **Repurpose aggressively** — A blog post becomes a thread becomes a newsletter becomes a video
4. **Review monthly** — What got engagement? Do more of that. What flopped? Drop it.
5. **Protect the cadence** — Missing a week is fine. Missing a month kills momentum.
