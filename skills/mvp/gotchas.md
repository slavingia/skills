# MVP Gotchas

Common failure modes when building an MVP. Review before advising.

## Scope Traps

- **Building features for "someday"** — only build what's proven manually. If you haven't done it by hand at least 5 times, you don't know enough to automate it.
- **Building for scale** — you don't have scale problems yet. Solve for 10 customers, not 10,000.
- **"Ship in a weekend" doesn't mean build something bad** — it means ruthlessly scope down. One feature, done well, that someone will pay for.

## Technology Traps

- **Building a mobile app when a website works** — mobile adds app store review, two platforms, and weeks of delay. A responsive website ships today.
- **Writing code when a spreadsheet works** — Google Sheets + Zapier + Stripe covers 80% of early-stage product needs. Code is a last resort.
- **Hiring an engineer when no-code tools suffice** — Carrd, Gumroad, Airtable, Notion, Tally, Zapier. Exhaust these before writing a line of code.

## Mindset Traps

- **Skipping the manual stage** — this is where you learn what actually matters. Every shortcut here becomes a wrong assumption baked into your product.
- **Polishing before shipping** — CraigsList has never been pretty. Neither was early Gumroad. Ship ugly, learn fast, polish what works.
- **Firecrawl/Perplexity results show funded competitors** — don't be intimidated. Most apps are just forms and lists. Competitors validate the market exists. Your advantage is community focus and speed, not features.
