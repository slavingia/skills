# Pricing Gotchas

Common failure modes when recommending or setting prices. Review before every pricing conversation.

## Behavioral Traps

- **Free as default** — Massive difference between free and $1 (zero price effect, Dan Ariely). People will line up for free brownies but the line disappears at 1 cent. Free means you learn nothing about willingness to pay.
- **Underpricing from impostor syndrome** — Start low, but always have a plan and timeline to raise. "I'll charge more later" without a trigger is a trap.
- **Confusing marketing with giving away product** — Ad-driven or "free forever" models make it exponentially harder to introduce charging later. Users anchor on free.

## Strategy Mistakes

- **Permanent pricing mindset** — Pricing is iterative, not permanent. Treat it like product features: ship, learn, adjust. Don't agonize over the "right" price on day one.
- **Cost-based when value-based fits better** — Software and digital products should almost always be value-priced. Your cost to serve an extra user is near-zero; price on the value they receive.
- **Competitor prices are anchors, not limits** — If your value proposition is different, your price should be too. Competitors inform positioning but don't dictate your ceiling or floor.

## Operational Oversights

- **Not accounting for payment processing fees** — Stripe charges 2.9% + 30 cents per transaction. On a $5 product, that's ~9% gone. On $1, it's 33%. Factor this into margin calculations, especially for low price points.
- **Firecrawl competitor pricing scrapes may hit paywalls** — Try scraping company pricing pages directly (e.g., `example.com/pricing`) rather than SaaS directories or review sites. Pricing pages are usually public; aggregator data is often gated or stale.
